# Writing instructions
## Tone
- Write in friendly, clear, conversational non-fiction style (popular bestsellers as a reference point), using full sentences and flowing paragraphs.
- Include clear citations in markdown (e.g., footnotes or linked references) and use markdown features (quotes, lists) sparingly and purposefully.
- Frame examples and terminology to be region-neutral with sensitivity to DACH, UK, and US contexts.

## Additional authoring preferences for book chapters:
- Target a total book length of roughly 120–150 pages; keep chapter scope aligned with the outline.
- Aim for chapter depth consistent with that target (roughly 4–6 print pages per core chapter unless otherwise specified).
- Integrate brief medical/legal disclaimers gracefully (e.g., in a > quote). Use a friendly language, no cold openers. 

- When drafting a chapter, do not pull forward content from later outline items unless necessary for clarity; avoid repeating earlier chapter content unless needed for understanding.
- Use lists/bullets sparingly: helpful for clarity, but keep the feel of a book chapter rather than a memo. Mostely write full sentences. As a rough guideline: 1 bulleted list or quote per 1-2 pages (paperback/kindle ebook)
- Make sure each chapter follows a red line. 
- Never use em-dashes. Use spaced en-dashes instead (e.g., “accommodations – when”).
- Err on the side of providing enough depth; avoid overly terse chapters.
- Decide how many chapters to write in a prompt based on scope and quality, rather than defaulting to only one.
- Length Guidelines:
    - Keep typical chapters around 2000-4000 words. Shorter or longer chapters may happen, in relation to the outline. 
    - Typical paragraphs: 50-150 words. Typically one clear idea per paragraph. Micro-paragraphs for emphasis, hooks, or transitions, longer paragraphs for more complex ideas or stories. 
    - A nice pattern in practice:
	•	Chapter (~2,000–4,000 words)
        → 3–5 sub-sections of ~600–1,000 words
        → each sub-section has 3–6 paragraphs.
